Workings for the calculation questions
Q44-Q50: Sensitivity = 90/100 = 90%. Specificity = 840/900 = 93.3%. PPV = 90/150 = 60%. NPV = 840/850 = 98.8%. False-negative rate = 10/100 = 10%. Accuracy = (90 + 840)/1,000 = 93%. False-positive rate = 60/900 = 6.7%.
Q51: Sensitivity = 70/100 = 70%. Specificity = 80/100 = 80%. Accuracy = (70 + 80)/200 = 75%.
Q52-Q53: True negatives = 1,800 - 90 = 1,710, so specificity = 1,710/1,800 = 95%. PPV = 180/(180 + 90) = 66.7%.
Q54-Q55: Non-infected = 5,000 - 250 = 4,750, so false-positive percentage = 475/4,750 = 10%. PPV = 225/(225 + 475) = 225/700 = 32.1%.
Q56-Q57: At 2% prevalence: TP = 1,800 and FP = 5% of 98,000 = 4,900, so PPV = 1,800/6,700 = 27%. At 20% prevalence: TP = 18,000 and FP = 5% of 80,000 = 4,000, so PPV = 18,000/22,000 = 82%. The same test gives a far better yield in a high-risk group.
Q88-Q89: Surveillance sensitivity = a/(a + c) = 60/80 = 75%. PPV = a/(a + b) = 80/100 = 80%.
Short rationales for selected questions
Q17: High-risk screening raises the prevalence in the screened group, which raises PPV and cuts false positives.
Q18 and Q21: Meningococcal meningitis is acute with a very short pre-clinical period, so it is not suitable for screening.
Q39: PPV rises with prevalence while sensitivity and specificity stay fixed.
Q75: Availability of an effective vaccine falls under 'ability to prevent, control or treat', not under public health importance.
Q101 and Q110: Eradication means zero worldwide incidence with the agent still held in laboratories; extinction means the agent is gone from nature and laboratories, and has not been achieved for any disease.
Q116: Airborne spread by droplets, helped by crowding, makes meningococcal meningitis hard to control by breaking transmission.
Q136: IPV gives humoral immunity but low gut immunity, so it is not useful for controlling epidemics.
Q138: The type 2 component of tOPV caused over 90% of cVDPV cases and about 40% of VAPP cases, and interfered with types 1 and 3.
Q166 and Q213: Doses and intervals follow your course notes (rifampin 10 mg/kg every 12 hours for 2 days from 1 month of age, maximum 600 mg per dose; booster for a major or contaminated wound if the last dose was 5 or more years ago).
Q184 and Q185: Chronic typhoid carriers sustain transmission; for meningococcal disease, post-vaccination immunity is NOT solid, so option D in Q185 is the non-challenge.
